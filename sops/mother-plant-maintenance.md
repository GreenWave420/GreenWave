# SOP: Mother Plant Maintenance

**Doc ID:** SOP-MOM-001 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Version:** 1.0 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Owner:** GM / Cultivation
**Effective date:** ____________ &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Review due:** annually

---

## 1. Purpose
To keep healthy, true-to-type mother (stock) plants in a stable vegetative state
as a clean, reliable source of genetically identical clones — the foundation of
every future crop.

## 2. Scope
All mother/stock plants and the mother room. Clone-taking is covered here through
to insertion into propagation; rooting is in the propagation workflow.

## 3. Why mothers get priority
Mothers are the **highest-value plants** in the facility — a pest, disease or
viroid (e.g. HpLVd) in a mother spreads to every clone taken from it. They get
the first, cleanest attention every day and the strictest hygiene.

## 4. Environment (confirm to your genetics)
| Parameter | Target |
|---|---|
| Photoperiod | 18/6 (or 24/0) — **must stay vegetative** |
| Day temp | 22–26 °C |
| Night temp | 20–22 °C |
| RH | 55–65 % |
| CO₂ | 800–1200 ppm |
| VPD | 0.8–1.1 kPa |
| Feed | Vegetative nutrient recipe, pH 5.8–6.2 |

- Never let light interruptions push mothers toward flowering.
- Keep mothers slightly under-driven (steady, not maximum growth) for longevity.

## 5. Daily tasks
- Inspect each mother: colour, turgor, new growth, signs of stress, deficiency.
- **Scout closely for pests/disease** — undersides of leaves, nodes, sticky
  traps. Mothers are the first line to catch an infestation.
- Confirm feed and light correct; check media moisture.
- Remove any dead/yellowing leaves and debris to waste.
- Log any cuttings taken and any abnormality.

## 6. Weekly tasks
- **Prune & shape:** remove lower/inner growth to keep an open structure, improve
  airflow and encourage strong clone sites. Don't over-strip.
- **Take clones** per propagation schedule (see §8).
- Rotate plant positions for even light exposure.
- Spot-test media EC/pH; flush if salts building up.
- Defoliate lightly for airflow; clear all trimmings.
- Assess vigour; flag tired plants for retirement.

## 7. Monthly tasks
- Review the **mother register**: age, health, and the yield/quality history of
  crops grown from each mother.
- Retire and replace declining mothers — always propagate the replacement
  *before* retiring, so genetics are never lost.
- Verify tags/labels match the genetic register exactly.
- Assess pathogen/viroid risk; schedule testing (esp. HpLVd) if growth is
  stunted, asymmetric or yield is dropping.
- Confirm a genetic back-up exists (preserved cuttings / tissue culture / seed).

## 8. Taking clones (cuttings)
1. **Sanitise** hands, gloves, blades/scissors and work surface. Sterilise the
   cutting tool **between every plant** (isopropyl or heat) to avoid spreading
   disease.
2. Select healthy, non-flowering shoots 10–15 cm with 2–3 nodes.
3. Cut at ~45° below a node with a clean, sharp blade.
4. Trim lower leaves; optionally dip in rooting hormone.
5. Insert into moistened, clean media (rockwool/plug/aerocloner).
6. Place under dome/low light, high humidity (see propagation).
7. **Label immediately**: genetics, mother ID, date, quantity → clone batch.
8. Record cuttings taken in the tracking system; keep counts reconciled.

## 9. Hygiene rules specific to mothers
- Enter the mother room **first**, before any dirtier room, or after full
  re-gowning/sanitising.
- Never bring tools from flower/harvest into the mother room without
  sterilising.
- Isolate any suspect mother immediately; do **not** take clones from a plant
  showing pest/disease/viroid symptoms.

## 10. Records
- Mother register (ID, genetics, date established, source, health notes).
- Clone log (date, mother ID, quantity, operator).
- Retirement/replacement records with destruction reference.
