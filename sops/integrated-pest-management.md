# SOP: Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

**Doc ID:** SOP-IPM-001 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Version:** 1.0 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Owner:** IPM lead / GM
**Effective date:** ____________ &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Review due:** annually

---

## 1. Purpose
To prevent, detect early, and control pests and diseases with the least-toxic
effective methods, protecting crop health and keeping product free of prohibited
residues.

## 2. Scope
All plants and rooms, incoming genetics, propagation, and the facility envelope
(entry points, screens, drains).

## 3. The IPM hierarchy (use in this order)
1. **Exclusion / prevention** — keep pests out and conditions unfavourable.
2. **Monitoring / scouting** — find problems while small.
3. **Cultural / physical** — sanitation, airflow, removal of affected material.
4. **Biological** — beneficial insects / microbes.
5. **Least-toxic chemical** — only approved products, rotated, as a last resort.

## 4. Prevention & exclusion
- Strict hygiene and directional workflow (see Cleaning SOP): clean → dirty.
- **Quarantine all incoming plants/genetics** and inspect before introduction.
- Insect screens on intakes; seal gaps; maintain positive/filtered airflow.
- Control humidity and airflow to suppress mould/mildew (biggest disease risks).
- Treat drains and eliminate standing water (fungus gnats breed there).
- No outdoor plants, soil or un-inducted people/pets into production areas.
- Dedicated PPE; sanitise between rooms so staff don't vector pests.

## 5. Monitoring & scouting
- **Daily:** visual scan of every room; check leaf undersides and sticky traps.
- **Weekly:** systematic grid scout of each room; count & change sticky traps;
  log catch numbers per room; map hotspots.
- Record findings every time (room, pest/disease, severity, location, date,
  scout). Trends matter more than single readings.

### Common threats to watch
| Pest / disease | Watch for |
|---|---|
| Spider mites | Stippling, fine webbing, dots on leaf undersides |
| Thrips | Silvery streaks, black specks |
| Fungus gnats | Adults at media surface / traps; larvae in wet media |
| Aphids | Clusters on new growth, sticky honeydew |
| Whitefly | White flyers when disturbed |
| Powdery mildew | White powdery patches on leaves |
| Botrytis (bud rot) | Grey mould, browning inside dense colas |
| Root rot (pythium) | Wilting, brown slimy roots, low DO in res |
| HpLVd (viroid) | Stunting, brittle stems, reduced potency/yield |

## 6. Action thresholds & response
- **None / trace:** continue monitoring; reinforce prevention.
- **Low:** targeted cultural/physical + biological control; increase scouting
  frequency on the affected room.
- **Action needed:** notify IPM lead & GM; isolate/treat; consider culling and
  destroying affected plants (recorded); apply approved treatment.
- **Disease (mould/viroid):** remove and destroy affected material immediately;
  do not take clones from affected plants; review environment.

## 7. Treatment rules
- Use **only products approved** for your licence, at label rate and interval.
- **Rotate modes of action** to prevent resistance.
- Observe pre-harvest intervals (PHI) — never treat where residue would exceed
  limits or contaminate near-harvest flower.
- Prefer preventive/biological programs over reactive spraying.
- Record every application: product, rate, area, date, operator, PHI.
- Keep SDS and approvals on file; store chemicals/biologicals per label, check
  expiries monthly.

## 8. Biological controls (if used)
- Order, store and release beneficials per supplier guidance.
- Introduce preventively before pest outbreaks where practical.
- Don't apply incompatible chemicals that would kill beneficials.

## 9. Records & review
- IPM logbook: scouting results, trap counts, treatments, releases, culls.
- **Monthly review:** trend analysis, treatment efficacy, resistance rotation,
  inventory & expiry reconciliation, update next month's plan.
